Where this could take you

What could your work actually look like?

01

Study people at work

Design better interviews, assess leadership or use employee data to understand what helps teams thrive. Explore talent management, employee experience and people analytics.

02

Help teams through change

Plan how employees hear about a reorganization, prepare managers for new roles, and measure whether people adopt the changes. Explore change management and organizational development.

03

Work on mergers & acquisitions

Before a deal closes, assess workforce risks, compensation and retention. After close, help combine teams, onboard employees and coordinate HR changes. Explore HR integration and human capital advisory.

Before the deal closes

The questions are about risk: which key people might leave, what compensation and benefits the two companies promise, and how different the cultures really are.

After it closes

The work is about people landing well: combining teams, onboarding employees, coordinating HR changes, and telling everyone clearly who reports to whom.

Something you could try before committing to school: Ask to own a piece of a reorganization, onboarding redesign or HR systems rollout. For U.S. clients, look for cross-border projects and exposure to compensation, benefits and workforce planning alongside local specialists. See what HR integration involves ↗

In case you want to do your own thing

Okay, but what if you want to be your own boss?

Ash, I kept thinking about what you said about having your own practice. That could mean a few very different things. The easiest way to sort it out: who would you want calling you, and what would you want them asking for help with?

“Our teams just merged. Help.”

HR integration or change consulting. Companies could hire you to help onboard an acquired team, plan employee communications, or figure out why everyone’s confused about who they report to. Start with a change course and try owning a piece of an integration, reorg, or HR systems rollout at work. Later, you could subcontract to a consulting firm or take on a project directly.

See what integration work looks like ↗

“Something’s off with how we work.”

Organizational development or talent consulting. You could help employers redesign hiring, run employee surveys, develop managers, or figure out how to make teams work better. Your hiring experience gives you a first service you could try: an interview audit and a better scorecard. An applied I/O master’s is worth exploring if you want to go deeper into assessment and research.

See where applied I/O can take you ↗

“I need help figuring out how to lead.”

Executive or team coaching. You might work one-on-one with new managers, coach teams after a restructure, or run leadership workshops. If the conversations are what you’d love most, compare coach training and supervised practice with the I/O route. Coaching and therapy are different services.

Explore UBC organizational coaching ↗

“I want help with my mental health.”

Clinical or counselling practice. If you want to see clients for therapy, look into clinical or counselling training and the specific credential you’d need. An I/O master’s or change certificate does not by itself qualify you as a psychologist or therapist. The psychologist title is protected in B.C., and the provincial regulator says psychotherapy licensure is scheduled to take effect November 29, 2027, with the application process expected to open in summer 2027 (both confirmed on its pages on Nov 16, 2026).

UBC counselling psychology ↗ · UBC clinical psychology ↗ · B.C. protected titles ↗ · B.C. psychotherapy updates ↗

Could mergers be your way in? Yep.

One possible route: hiring and HR → help with an integration → move into organizational development or people consulting → take on your own clients if you like it. What you learn on a deal (spotting where cultures clash, supporting leaders, and helping employees find their footing) can carry over to reorganizations and everyday corporate work. If workplace psychology is the part you’re already excited about, you can also go straight toward talent assessment, employee experience, or people analytics. No merger required. Except the snack-budget one, obviously.

My vote for a first experiment: try a change project if you’re curious about mergers, or redesign one part of hiring if you’re curious about I/O. If working for yourself sticks as an idea, start with one service you can describe in a sentence. Before taking U.S. clients, check the rules that apply to your services, along with contracts, insurance, and employee data handling. B.C.’s guide to setting up on your own ↗

The money and who might hire you

Okay, but does it pay for snacks?

I pulled a few real numbers so you don’t have to guess. Job Bank gives you the big picture around Vancouver (its wage pages were last updated in November 2025); the Deloitte numbers come from specific Canadian job ads. Named companies below are places to look, not promises of an opening. If a role catches your eye, check whether it’s still open and whether you could do it from here.

Workplace psychology, talent & people analytics

Pay: One organization and work transformation consultant posting listed $58k–$102k base (the page was still live on Nov 16, 2026). That’s a consulting example; for in-house work, compare the Job Bank HR wage data with actual postings.

Where to look: People teams at large retailers like lululemon, banks, tech companies and universities; assessment vendors and consultancies if you’d prefer multiple clients.

Search: Talent management specialist, people analytics analyst, employee experience specialist, assessment consultant.

Change management & organizational development

Pay: The Lower Mainland’s business management consulting benchmark is $48/hour median (about $100k/year at 40 hours/week). The low and high benchmarks are $28.75 and $74.52/hour. This group includes more than change management.

Where to look: Health authorities like Vancouver Coastal Health, universities, utilities, telecoms, and consulting firms such as Deloitte. Large systems rollouts and reorganizations are good projects to look for.

Search: Change analyst, organizational development specialist, change management consultant, HR transformation.

HR integration & the people side of M&A

Pay: Deloitte examples listed $84k–$126k for a senior post-merger integration consultant and $101k–$169k for a human capital M&A manager (both pages were live on Nov 16, 2026, though a live page doesn’t prove the role is still taking applications). These are experienced roles and the listed ranges exclude potential bonuses.

Where to look: Deal advisory practices like PwC Deals and Mercer M&A advisory, plus in-house integration teams at companies that buy other businesses. For U.S. client work, check whether the role can be based in Canada and how much travel it requires.

Search: HR integration, people integration, human capital due diligence, deal transformation. Add “HR” or “people” to avoid getting mostly finance jobs.

Coaching, consulting, or your own practice

Pay: For an in-house role, look up learning and development or leadership development salaries. Independent work has no fixed salary: as an illustration, $125 × 15 billable hours × 46 weeks = $86,250 in gross annual revenue. That rate is hypothetical, not a market estimate. Subtract expenses and plan for unpaid time finding clients, cancellations, taxes, and benefits.

Where to look: Leadership development teams, coaching firms, HR consulting boutiques, or employers who might hire you directly for one project. If you go independent, the company buying the service is your client.

Search: Leadership development specialist, learning and development consultant, team coach, HR consultant.

Clinical or counselling practice

Pay: Lower Mainland Job Bank medians are $37.94/hour for the counselling therapist occupation group (about $79k/year) and $48.53/hour for the counselling psychologist group (about $101k/year). Private session fees are business revenue and don’t tell you take-home pay.

Where to look: Health authorities, community mental health nonprofits, group clinics, universities and employee assistance providers. This is a separate training and credential route.

Search: Clinical counsellor, mental health therapist, psychologist. Check the B.C. credential links first.

For the hourly jobs, I did the math at 40 hours a week for 52 weeks. Your actual pay depends on the hours, benefits, and any bonus. Job Bank figures are broad medians for an area, a job ad is one employer’s offer, and a freelancer’s fees are revenue before expenses and taxes. They aren’t the same thing.
